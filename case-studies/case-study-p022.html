AI Executive Assistant Control Center

The human side of an AI executive assistant. Scheduled AI runs triage a CEO's inbox and calendar every hour, and this web app is where a person reviews what they prepared, approves it, or steers the next run. Nothing sends on its own.

The Problem

At an executive coaching firm, scheduled AI runs were already doing real work for the CEO: triaging his inbox, watching his calendar, drafting replies and proposing meeting times. But everything they produced landed as rows in Notion databases. Reviewing it meant digging through pages, and a card made sense only to whoever had followed the thread.

The team needed one place to see what the AI had prepared, decide quickly, and trust that nothing would go out without a person saying yes.

How It Works

Runs prepare workEvery hour, scheduled Claude runs triage the inbox, watch the calendar, draft replies and propose scheduling. Each writes a card with context, a due time and what it proposes to do.
Shared recordThe runs and the app never talk to each other. Notion is the shared record: the app writes a person's decision into the same fields the runs already read, so the runs needed no changes at all.
ReviewOne list sorted by urgency (Now, Today, This week). Titles start with a verb and quote the actual ask, and every card explains who it is about, what happened, what is done and what is needed, with links to the real draft or thread.
ActApprove, pick an option, redo with a note, snooze, comment or mark obsolete. Each action can be undone until the next run picks it up, and items clear themselves once the AI confirms the work is done.
SchedulingA status table for every open scheduling thread, with the CEO's own rules enforced, such as no client sessions on Mondays or Fridays.
Run healthA strip shows whether each scheduled run is on time, quiet, or ended with a warning, so a silent failure never hides.

Designed With the CEO, Not For Him

The interface went through three directions. The first was a calm, clean console. The second was a warm, lamp-lit desk theme with a clear color language and playful touches: a gamified first-use tutorial, badges and an animated lobby. It also had separate inbox-shadowing screens for the CEO's email, his assistant's inbox and his text messages, each designed around how that inbox actually works.

Then the CEO reviewed it. He wanted a plain task manager: one list by urgency, full control over every item, items that clear themselves, and titles that say exactly what to do. Inbox screens became simple warnings about drafts going stale, since the drafts themselves are better handled in the email client.

The third version is organized by who acts next: Needs me, Needs the CEO, Follow up, Calibrate, and Machines. It kept the color language and cut the decoration. The little personality that remains is tied to live data, like an indicator that only lights up when something truly cannot wait.

Color tells you whose move it is. Amber is yours, lilac is what the AI did, green is settled. If something is amber, a person can act on it.

Safe by Design

Nothing sends
The AI and the app only write drafts and record decisions. People review and send.
Four fields only
The app can write a decision, a note to the run, a step and guidance. Nothing else.
Live re-check
Every click is checked against the live record before anything is written.
No accidental schema changes
The app refuses to write a value that is not already an allowed option.
Full audit trail
Every action is logged with who, what and when.
One kill switch
A single setting turns every action button off instantly.

Outcome & Impact

  • One place to steer the AI: everything the hourly runs prepare shows up as a clear list of decisions.
  • Decisions in seconds: each card carries its own context, so anyone on the team can act without the backstory.
  • Zero risk of the AI sending on its own: drafts only, with every action audited and reversible until the next run.
  • Shaped by its user: the CEO's feedback turned a decorative concept into a tool built around who acts next.
  • Status: v1 is live and demoed to the CEO. The v3 redesign is in build.
