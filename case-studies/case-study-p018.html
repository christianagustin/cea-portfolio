Executive Workforce & Recruitment Dashboard

Turned a hand-maintained, 21-tab Excel workbook and eight recruiter sheets into an hourly-refreshed management dashboard with month-on-month comparisons, a recruitment funnel, and a weekly emailed report, without asking the client to change how they already work.

The Problem

A manpower agency tracks deployed staff, attendance, hires and attrition for a dozen client sites in a large Excel workbook that its operations team updates by hand every day. Management wanted reports that compare this week to last week and this month to last month, and proof that automation was producing insight beyond what manual reporting could.

Rebuilding their process around a new database was not an option. The dashboard had to read the workbook exactly as it is.

How It Works

Read as-isEvery hour, n8n downloads the live workbook and a single code node parses all 21 tabs in one pass. Tabs are matched by fuzzy pattern, because names drift month to month (spacing, capitalization).
CleanThe parser handles headers that never sit in row 1, future dates pre-filled as zeros, rows being edited mid-day, and Excel serial dates, with sanity floors that reject half-filled rows.
ComputeHeadcount against target per site, attendance rate, hires against attrition, attrition expressed as a rate, month-on-month deltas, and full daily history so any date range can be explored.
Recruitment funnelEight recruiter sheets are merged into one view: assigned, interviewed, assessed, orientation. It shows the team funnel, each recruiter's funnel and monthly cohorts, with recruiters anonymized by number at the client's request.
PublishEach engine writes its own JSON snapshot to a self-hosted server, so one failing source never blanks the whole page. A static dashboard behind HTTPS and login reads the snapshots, with a period picker that drives every chart.
Weekly reportEvery Monday an HTML card email goes to management. Code computes every number. The AI only writes the short narrative from those numbers and never does arithmetic.

The Audit That Reframed the Conversation

Once the data was flowing, I audited the dashboard the way a decision-maker would read it. Two panels that should have agreed did not reconcile, which raised a definitions question to settle with the client before anyone acted on it. More importantly, attrition had only ever been shown as a count. Shown as a monthly rate, it revealed that a large share of new hiring was simply replacing people who left.

That shifted the ROI conversation from "how many leads are coming in" to retention, which is where the real constraint was. Captions on the dashboard are rule-based and adapt to the live numbers, so the insight stays current without anyone rewriting it.

A dashboard earns its keep when it changes the question management is asking, not when it looks good.

Outcome & Impact

  • Management gets comparative reporting on any period, refreshed every hour, with no manual compilation.
  • The client kept their workflow: the operations team still updates the same workbook they always have.
  • Recruitment is measurable end to end, per recruiter and per monthly cohort.
  • A weekly report arrives on its own every Monday, with numbers computed in code and a plain-language summary.
  • The audit surfaced retention as the real lever, changing what leadership focuses on.
